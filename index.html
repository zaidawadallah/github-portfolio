About Me

I am a senior at Lamar University in Beaumont, Texas, completing a Bachelor of Science in Computer Information Sciences, with graduation expected in December 2026. My coursework has given me a foundation in programming, discrete mathematics and computer networking. In Computer Networks I worked on a team that used the ns-3 simulator to compare TCP Reno and NewReno congestion control, which taught me how much careful experiment design matters when you want numbers you can trust.

Outside the classroom I co-founded Swift Towing and Recovery, a towing and roadside-assistance company in Rosharon, south of Houston, in March 2026. Running it means handling dispatch, pricing, customer communication, phone systems and online advertising myself. It is also where most of my software ideas come from. My AI project, Swift Dispatch AI, exists because late-night customer texts are short and messy, and a dispatcher has to re-ask the same questions every time.

My technical interests are applied AI, networking and systems, and automation that saves people time. I enjoy taking something apart to see how it works, whether that is a TCP trace, a desktop PC I built and upgraded, or the engine of a vehicle I am repairing to resell. I also build analysis tools for my own futures-trading research, which has taught me to test ideas with data before trusting them.

My career goal is an entry-level software developer, IT support or systems/network administrator role in the Houston area, where I can grow into a developer who builds reliable tools for operations teams. Over the next year I plan to earn the CompTIA Network+ and AWS Cloud Practitioner certifications, strengthen my SQL and cloud skills, and keep shipping small, well-tested projects that solve real problems.

Projects

Project 1 · AI Assistant Project

Swift Dispatch AI

A tow-request intake assistant. Paste a customer's text or voicemail and it returns a structured dispatch ticket (service, vehicle, pickup, drop-off, urgency and safety flags), an itemized estimate and a ready-to-send SMS reply. Claude extracts the facts through a forced tool call with a JSON schema; a separate, tested pricing engine calculates the quote so the AI can never invent a price. An offline rule-based engine provides a no-key demo and an automatic fallback.

Project 2 · Course Project (CPSC 4317 Computer Networks)

TCP Reno vs NewReno in ns-3

A three-person team project using the ns-3 network simulator. We traced the TCP congestion window over a bottleneck link, compared Reno with NewReno (about 9.45 vs 9.92 Mbps) and measured how bottleneck buffer size affects throughput and queuing delay. I found that two experiments used different SACK settings, which made Reno look far worse than it was, and re-ran every case under identical settings.

Project 3 · Personal Project

ORB Backtest Analyzer

A Python command-line tool that compares variants of an opening-range-breakout futures strategy from a trade log. It reports win rate, the breakeven win rate implied by average win and loss, expectancy per trade, profit factor, max drawdown and a weekday split, and it warns when the sample is too small to trust. It replaced a spreadsheet where I kept making arithmetic mistakes.
